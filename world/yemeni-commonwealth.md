## What it is (one line)

Yemen is a permanent constituent state of Nelôxia, analogous to **Hawaii** in constitutional status
but carrying some of the cultural and administrative autonomy of the **Northern Mariana Islands** —
a Hawaii–CNMI hybrid. It is the federation's Red Sea and Indian Ocean platform, and Aden is its
southern counterpart to Kunislinnô.

- **Constitutional name:** *The Yemeni Commonwealth of Nelôxia*
- **Nelôxian civil name:** *Jemenin Ühismaa* ("the Yemeni common-land")
- **Internationally recognized local name:** Yemen

## Constitutional status

The Commonwealth:

- is **permanently part of Nelôxia**;
- **elects members to the federal legislature** (the Diēt) and participates in presidential and
  federal elections — unlike Guam, it is **fully represented federally**;
- **retains its own constitution, courts, civil law, and local official languages**;
- **controls** education, land tenure, religion, municipal government, and cultural policy;
- **shares** defense, currency, citizenship, foreign affairs, customs, and major infrastructure
  with the federal state.

Unlike an ordinary province it has constitutionally protected autonomy; unlike Guam it is fully
represented. It is therefore closer to a **Hawaii–CNMI hybrid** than to any single model.

- **Official local languages:** Arabic and Nelôxi
- **Federal working languages:** Nelôxi and English

## Historical path

### The Aden Compact, 1870s–1910s

Nelôxian shipping interests established their first permanent facilities around Aden as the state
extended its trade routes between the Baltic, the Mediterranean, East Africa, India, and Southeast
Asia. Rather than occupying the whole territory, Nelôxia negotiated **port, railway, water, and
customs concessions** with coastal authorities. Aden became the southern anchor of the Nelôxian
maritime system. Nelôxian engineers built:

- desalination and water-storage systems;
- a deep-water commercial harbor;
- railway links into the Yemeni interior;
- public hospitals and technical colleges;
- bonded warehouses and international financial facilities.

The territory drew Nelôxians, Yemenis, Somalis, Ethiopians, Indians, Arabs from across the Red Sea,
and merchants from the wider Nelosphere.

### The Yemeni Federal Movement

During the collapse of the Ottoman and European imperial orders, Yemeni politics divided between
monarchy, regional independence, and incorporation into larger Arab states. A separate **federal
movement** emerged around Aden, Taiz, and the port cities, arguing that association with Nelôxia
offered constitutional autonomy, protection from foreign annexation, access to Nelôxian capital and
infrastructure, guaranteed local representation, and freedom of movement throughout the Nelôxian
state. After a prolonged civil conflict, the federal movement controlled most of the country's
principal cities and transport corridors.

### Accession — the Treaty of Aden

Yemen entered Nelôxia through the **Treaty of Aden**, initially as a **self-governing associated
commonwealth**. After a **twenty-year transition and two referendums**, it became a permanent
constituent state. The accession was legally a **negotiated federation** between the Nelôxian state
and the Yemeni Commonwealth — not a conquest.

## Yemen's alternate development

Federal membership radically changes Yemen's twentieth-century trajectory. Instead of prolonged
isolation, fragmentation, and infrastructure collapse, Yemen becomes Nelôxia's principal Red Sea
and Indian Ocean platform. Federal investment concentrates on water, electricity, housing, ports,
education, public health, and transport.

By the present day:

```text
Population:      ≈ 38 million
Nominal GDP:     ≈ $720 billion
GDP per capita:  ≈ $19,000
Capital:         Sana'a (commonwealth capital)
Federal gateway: Greater Aden (≈ 6.5 million) — largest city
Other major cities: Taiz, Sana'a, Hodeidah, Mukalla, Ibb
```

**Aden** becomes one of the world's major port, shipping-insurance, logistics, energy-trading, and
maritime-law centers — the southern counterpart to Kunislinnô. Kunislinnô faces the Baltic and
northern finance; **Aden faces the Red Sea, East Africa, and the Indian Ocean**, and holds the
watch over the Bab-el-Mandeb.

## Settlements (naming layer)

Local names are kept in **Arabic** (an official language of the Commonwealth), so the region's
toponymy is the map's Arabic layer — distinct from the Finnic, Germanic, Slavic, Romance, and
Ottoman/Greek layers to the north. Principal settlements: **Aden** (federal gateway), **Sana'a**
(capital), **Taiz**, **Hodeidah** (Al Hudaydah), **Ibb**, **Mukalla**, **Dhamar**, **Seiyun**
(Say'un, Wadi Hadhramaut), **Ataq**, **Zinjibar**, and **Hadibu** on Socotra. Where a Nelôxi civic
overlay exists it sits beside — never over — the Arabic name; Aden's role as the southern federal
gateway is administrative, not a renaming.

## Relationship to the rest of the state

The Commonwealth is Region 12 of the twelve-region federation. It shares the currency, the courts
of appeal above its own, citizenship, the customs union, foreign affairs, and defense; it keeps its
constitution, its civil and religious law, its land tenure, and its languages. It is the clearest
expression of the state's founding principle — **incorporate without erasing** — carried across an
ocean: a people that federated in, kept itself, and gained a continent-spanning market and a great
power's protection in exchange for a shared operating system.
