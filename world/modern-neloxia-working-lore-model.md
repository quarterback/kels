## Core frame

Modern Nelôxia is a maritime-infrastructure state built over roughly five centuries through
layered accessions, port compacts, corridor provinces, chartered regions, and — in its latest
phase — a western Alpine–Mediterranean industrial core, a southern Balkan corridor, and an
overseas constituent commonwealth on the Red Sea.

Its borders look unusual on a modern map because they are not ethnic borders. They are accumulated
jurisdictions: ports, rail basins, customs corridors, shipyards, energy terminals, naval stations,
financial courts, and treaty infrastructure. The country holds together through procedure,
logistics, law, finance, defense production, and language mediation.

The result is a state that began as a Baltic maritime power and became a **four-sea, two-ocean
system**: Baltic–Black Sea–Adriatic–Mediterranean, with an Arctic frontage on top and a
transoceanic Red Sea / Indian Ocean anchor at Aden.

## Geographic structure (the twelve regions, as systems)

### 1. The northern lobe (Region 1: Karelia & the North)

The Arctic/White Sea frontage and the Vīpôri isthmus. Boreal resources, the Baltic–White Sea
canal, radar and missile ranges, and the two-ocean transit route that bypasses the Danish straits.

### 2. The metropolitan Baltic core (Regions 2–4)

The historical center and a large share of the demographic mass: the Livonian islands and
southeastern interior, the Lithuanian spine, and the Prussian–Pomeranian shore belt from the
Curonian Spit to the Oder mouth. This is the archive core — language, court, navy, capital finance,
state memory — with the capital basin at **Kunislinnô**, the delta crown at Tantsika, and the Oder
gate at Stetīn, where Baltic maritime traffic enters the inland corridors.

### 3. The eastern corridor (Regions 5–6)

The Belarus–Lublin seam running south through Subcarpathia, Prešov, and Transcarpathia into the
Moldavian arc and the Black Sea littoral — Kôstônç at the Danube mouth, **Uusatôm** as the Black
Sea anchor. This is the Baltic–Black Sea axis: rail-gauge transfer (Volkovixi), customs law,
grain, and the river roads between the seas.

### 4. The Pannonian bridge & the Alpine–Adriatic arm (Regions 7–8)

The Hungarian land-bridge binds the eastern corridor to the western arm; the arm rises through
the Alpine engineering hinge at Gräts (tunnels, passes, hydrology, hardened logistics) and drops
to **Triest**, the legal-financial Adriatic port: maritime courts, shipping registries, bonded
warehouses, insurance, customs law, Mediterranean finance.

### 5. The Dalmatian coast (Region 9)

The Istrian peninsula, the Kvarner (**Fiume**, Pôla, Krk), the Adriatic seaboard — Zadar and
Šibenik's waters, Spalôt, Ragūz — and the Herzegovinian hinterland. The warm-water
naval-commercial shore: repair, island harbors, convoy organization.

### 6. The western Alpine–Riviera arc (Region 10) — *the industrial heart of the expanded state*

The Rhône–Riviera coast (Marselha, Niça, Tolon), the **Lombard–Piedmont basin** (Milano, Torino),
and the **Veneto–Adriatic manufacturing belt** (Venesia, Verona, Padova), with the southern Swiss
valleys (Lugano, Sion, Chur) at the passes. This is the single richest zone in the federation —
advanced manufacturing, design, machine tools, luxury and chemical industry, Mediterranean ports —
and it is why the expanded state jumps into the global top five.

### 7. The Thracian–Macedonian corridor (Region 11)

The southern Balkan land-bridge to the Aegean threshold: the Bulgarian south (Burgas on its
salt-lake bay), Eastern Thrace at Edirne, the southern Albanian coast (Valona), and the Macedonian
Vardar (Monastir, Veles) — agriculture, light industry, tobacco and mining, and the overland reach
toward the Aegean.

### 8. The Yemeni Commonwealth (Region 12) — *the transoceanic anchor*

The overseas constituent state on the Red Sea and the Indian Ocean, capital **Sana'a**, federal
gateway **Greater Aden** (≈6.5M). Aden is the southern counterpart to Kunislinnô: Kunislinnô faces
the Baltic and northern finance; Aden faces the Red Sea, East Africa, and the Indian Ocean —
one of the world's major port, shipping-insurance, logistics, energy-trading, and maritime-law
centers. *(Constitutional status and accession history below and in `neloxia-expanded-lore.md`.)*

### The federal city

**Sevastopol** — the great drowned-valley naval harbour — is a constitutionally distinct **federal
city and naval territory** (a capital-district model, comparable to Washington, D.C. or Gibraltar),
the seat of the Fleet, not folded into an ordinary region.

### Beyond the state

**Corsica** and the **Canary Islands** are members of the sovereign **Meridian States**
(`meridian-states.md`); **Atlanta** is a separate Atlantic country (`atlanta-lore.md`); **Skaria**
is the aligned Nordic breakaway partner (`skaria-lore.md`). None is counted in Nelôxia's totals.

For scale: a **great power** — territorially larger than any EU member, demographically on the
order of Russia or Japan, economically inside the global top five, between Germany and Japan in
the world order. The four largest economic zones are the **Lombard–Piedmont basin**, the **Baltic
financial core**, the **Rhône–Riviera coast**, and the **Veneto–Adriatic manufacturing belt**,
with the **Aden metropolitan corridor** as the transoceanic fifth.

**How the population number works.** The real-world base of the listed subdivisions is far below
≈148M; the canonical figure is the alternate history, not a miscount — the same argument
`neloxia-lore.md` §II makes for the core, at continental-plus-transoceanic scale: no partitions,
no Soviet-era stagnation and collapse, no EU-era emigration drain, an unbroken trade-and-industry
pull along the whole corridor for centuries, and — decisively — the incorporation of the Po-valley
industrial heartland and a Yemen that never fragmented but developed as the federation's Red Sea
platform. The premium concentrates where the state's history put it: the capital basin, the shore
belt, the corridor cities, the Lombard–Piedmont–Veneto core, and the Aden corridor.

**How the GDP number works.** The state reorganized some of Europe's most valuable economic
geography centuries earlier than in our timeline and never let it fragment — the Baltic logistics
system, Kunislinnô as a financial and customs hub, the re-drawn Amber Road freight corridor, the
Danube approaches, the Adriatic terminals, the Black Sea grain gateway, **the Po-valley industrial
core and the Riviera**, and the Aden maritime-finance corridor — plus the headquarters functions of
the whole Nelosphere (banking, insurance, maritime law, arbitration, standards, education,
professional services). Between Germany and Japan per unit of output: a wealthy industrial great
power, not an implausible petrostate.

## The three economic measures (canon)

1. **Domestic GDP** — ≈$6.4 trillion nominal: firmly inside the global top five, canonically ≈4th–5th.
2. **GDP per capita** — ≈$43,000: wealthy and industrial across the European regions; the Yemeni
   Commonwealth (≈$19,000) is developing fast off federal investment and pulls the average down
   while the Po-valley and Baltic cores pull it up.
3. **Nelosphere GDP** — the combined output of the countries using Nelôxi as a principal
   commercial, legal, or institutional language: **Atlanta**, the African bloc (**Soninka &
   Toro**, Adrāra), the **Meridian States**, **Valdória**, and the Baltic League partners —
   **Skaria** foremost. This figure is dramatically larger than the (already very large) domestic
   economy — the more interesting number: Britain's global weight was never Britain's GDP but
   English, the City, and common law; Nelôxia's is the language, the peg, the courts, and the
   counting-house standards. The pluricentric language (six seats, one spine) is this measure's
   institutional skeleton.

## Acquisition logic

Nelôxia's modern shape is cumulative. No single event explains the whole map.

- **Baltic origin.** The old state grows from maritime governance, court administration, port
  control, trade law, and Baltic sea power — the core of Regions 1–4.
- **Hanseatic accession.** The Pomeranian shore joins through port compacts, economic alignment,
  and revived maritime-commercial charters.
- **Corridor accession.** The eastern seam and the Pannonian bridge align through infrastructure
  sovereignty: Nelôxia provides capital, security, rail continuity, customs law, courts, fiscal
  trust, and access to the seas.
- **Adriatic incorporation.** Triest, the Istrian–Kvarner ports, and the Dalmatian coast become
  the southern terminals once the land-bridge matures.
- **Black Sea completion.** The Moldavian arc and the littoral bring the state to Uusatôm, and the
  naval city of **Sevastopol** is held as a federal district.
- **The western industrial accession.** The **Western Alpine & Riviera arc** — the Rhône–Riviera,
  Piedmont, Lombardy, and the Veneto — accedes as the Amber Road's western terminus matures into a
  full industrial core, adding the Po-valley manufacturing heartland and the Mediterranean ports.
- **The southern corridor.** The **Thracian–Macedonian corridor** joins as the overland reach to
  the Aegean threshold.
- **The transoceanic accession.** The **Yemeni Commonwealth** accedes through the **Aden Compact**
  (1870s–1910s) and the **Treaty of Aden**, first as a self-governing associated commonwealth and,
  after a twenty-year transition and two referendums, as a permanent constituent state — a
  negotiated federation, not a conquest (`neloxia-expanded-lore.md`).

## The Yemeni Commonwealth (constitutional summary)

The Yemeni Commonwealth is **permanently part of Nelôxia** — a constituent transoceanic state,
analogous to Hawaii in constitutional status but with the cultural and administrative autonomy of
the Northern Mariana Islands (a **Hawaii–CNMI hybrid**). Constitutional name: *The Yemeni
Commonwealth of Nelôxia*; Nelôxian civil name *Jemenin Ühismaa*; the internationally recognized
local name remains **Yemen**.

- **Permanently part of the federation**; elects members to the federal legislature; participates
  in presidential and federal elections.
- **Retains its own constitution, courts, civil law, and local official languages**; controls
  education, land tenure, religion, municipal government, and cultural policy.
- **Shares** defense, currency, citizenship, foreign affairs, customs, and major infrastructure
  with the federal state.
- **Scale:** ≈38 million; nominal GDP ≈$720 billion; GDP per capita ≈$19,000. Capital: **Sana'a**
  (commonwealth capital). Largest city and federal gateway: **Greater Aden** (≈6.5M). Major cities:
  Taiz, Sana'a, Hodeidah, Mukalla, Ibb. Official local languages: **Arabic and Nelôxi**; federal
  working languages: **Nelôxi and English**.

Federal investment (water, desalination, power, housing, ports, education, public health,
transport) makes Yemen the federation's principal Red Sea and Indian Ocean platform rather than a
fragmented periphery — Aden as the southern Kunislinnô.

## State model

Nelôxia is best understood as an accumulated jurisdiction state — a four-sea, two-ocean system
with corridor provinces, an industrial western core, and a transoceanic commonwealth. Its unity is
procedural rather than ethnic; its institutions make difference administratively useful.

Local languages remain alive across the state. Nelôxi functions as the platform language: courts,
customs, rail, ports, military, technical schools, shipping, insurance, finance, and
interregional administration.

## Language model inside the state

The local languages of the regions remain active: Karelian and Russian in the north; Estonian,
Latvian, and Lithuanian in the core; Polish, Kashubian, and German traditions on the shore;
Belarusian and Ukrainian down the seam; Romanian in the arc; Hungarian on the bridge; German,
Slovene, Friulian, and Italian in the Alpine–Adriatic arm; Croatian/Chakavian on the Dalmatian
coast; **French, Occitan, Italian, and Lombard in the western arc; Bulgarian, Turkish, Albanian,

## The Baltic League

Nelôxia founded and anchors the Baltic League — the state's main tool for organizing autonomy
outside NATO and the Euro while remaining central to European security and commerce.

Full members: **Nelôxia, Skaria, Denmark, Sweden, Norway, Finland**. Observer states: **Germany,
Poland, Russia**. The League's keystone is the **Baltic-entrance regime**: Skaria holds the
Kattegat/Öresund straits, and the two states run a functional condominium over Baltic access.
With the transoceanic expansion the League's maritime remit now stretches from the Baltic and
White Sea through the Black Sea and the Adriatic to the western Mediterranean and — via Aden — the
Red Sea and Indian Ocean.

| Pillar | Function |
|---|---|
| Customs and corridor law | shared freight, customs, port, and rail standards |
| Monetary clearing | settlement system independent of the Euro but tied to European trade |
| Defense procurement | joint standards, common production chains, coastal security programs |
| Maritime security | Baltic, White Sea, Black Sea, Adriatic, Mediterranean, Red Sea, Indian Ocean, and (with the Meridian realm) Atlantic coordination |

## Modern geopolitical posture

Nelôxia is not a small neutral state and not a subordinate alliance member. It is a non-NATO,
non-Euro great power: militarily interoperable with Europe, financially independent from the
Eurozone, and strong enough to make European security depend on Nelôxian ports, corridors, and
defense industry — and now on the Bab-el-Mandeb chokepoint at Aden.

It sells stability, rents access, controls standards, manufactures security, and refuses
absorption.
